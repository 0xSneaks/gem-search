Notes from the Orchard Web

By the Night Shift Naturalist · 6 min read

Every morning the pear trees hold a new map. Between two branches, an orb weaver has rebuilt the spider web she ate the night before, thread by thread, in under an hour.

Dew on an orb web at sunrise

Field notes

A web is a record of a walk. Each radial line is a trip from the hub to the frame and back, and every crossing is a decision about where to go next.

  • Three anchor threads carry most of the load.
  • Radials are laid first, spiral last.
  • The sticky spiral is spun from the outside in.

Why the hub matters

The hub is where the spider waits. Vibrations travel along the radials, so from the center she can tell which sector was touched and how hard.

Silk

Dragline silk is stronger than steel by weight. The spider trails a safety line behind her wherever she goes, which is why her path is always visible.

Macro view of silk strands

Orb webs

Orb webs are built in a fixed order. Watch one long enough and you can predict the next move.

  1. Bridge line across the gap.
  2. Frame and radials from the hub.
  3. Temporary spiral, then the capture spiral.

Reading a broken web

A torn sector tells you where prey hit. A clean hole in the middle tells you the spider left on purpose. Compare with the field notes.
